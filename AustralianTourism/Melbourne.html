About Melbourne

Melbourne is the capital city of Victoria and Australia's second-largest metropolis, home to an estimated 5.4 million people. Located on the southeastern coast around the head of Port Phillip Bay, it serves as the state's financial, administrative, and cultural epicenter. Routinely ranked among the world’s most liveable cities, Melbourne is globally celebrated for its sprawling Victorian-era architecture, extensive parklands, and a highly diverse, multicultural population.

Landmarks in Melbourne

Flinder's Street Station

is Australia's oldest train station and an iconic cultural landmark located on the corner of Flinders and Swanston Streets in the heart of Melbourne. Completed in its current grand form in 1910, the heritage-listed station is famous for its distinctive yellow facade, large green copper dome, and iconic row of departure clocks. Spanning more than two city blocks along the Yarra River, it serves as the primary hub for Melbourne’s metropolitan rail network and is the busiest suburban railway station in the Southern Hemisphere, handling over 1,500 trains and 110,000 commuters daily.

Melbourne Cricket Ground(MCG)

The Melbourne Cricket Ground, affectionately known as "the 'G," is Australia's largest and most iconic sports stadium. Established in 1853, this historic venue sits in Yarra Park just a short distance from the Melbourne city centre. It holds a massive capacity of over 100,000 spectators, making it one of the largest stadiums in the world. The ground serves as the spiritual home of Australian rules football in the winter and cricket during the summer months. It famously hosts massive annual events, including the AFL Grand Final and the traditional Boxing Day Test match. Visitors can easily access the stadium via nearby public transport options like Jolimont and Richmond train stations.
